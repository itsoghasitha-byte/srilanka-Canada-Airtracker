Passenger essentials

Safety basics everyone should know

General guidance only. Read the safety card on your aircraft and follow the operating crew.

01 · RestraintsLicensed photo · full image

Seat belt & seating

Fasten the belt low and snug. Keep it fastened whenever seated, especially when the sign is on or turbulence is possible.

02 · ExitsLicensed photo · full image

Locate your exits

Find the nearest exits before departure; an exit may be behind you. Follow exit signs and crew direction.

03 · OxygenLicensed photo · full image

Oxygen mask

If masks deploy, pull one toward you, cover your nose and mouth, secure it and breathe normally. Assist yourself first.

04 · Water safetyLicensed photo · full image

Life vest

Read the aircraft-specific safety card. On many aircraft, inflate the vest only after leaving the aircraft unless crew instruct otherwise.

05 · TurbulenceLicensed photo · full image

When turbulence hits

Return to your seat when instructed, buckle up, secure loose objects and keep aisles clear.

06 · EvacuationLicensed photo · full image

Leave bags behind

Follow crew commands and safe exit directions, leave belongings behind, and move away from the aircraft.

Crew roles · public overview

Meet the safety team

Visual role cards explain public-facing responsibilities, not confidential airline operating procedures.

Cabin crew member

Provides safety briefings, monitors the cabin, supports passengers, communicates concerns and follows approved procedures.

Passenger safety support

Helps passengers understand safety information, supports people who need assistance and reports hazards promptly.

Team communication

Calm communication and coordinated action matter. Crew follow airline-specific training and command structures.

Public guidance vs professional training

Know the difference

This independent project covers broadly known passenger precautions. It does not replace aircraft-specific safety cards, regulator requirements, airline manuals, or instructor-led drills.

Always defer to the operating crew. Procedures vary by aircraft and airline. During a real flight, follow the instructions given on board.
