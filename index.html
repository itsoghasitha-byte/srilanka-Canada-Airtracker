01 — 06 / SAFETY LIBRARY
01 · Restraints

Seat belt & seating

Fasten your belt low and snug. Keep it fastened whenever seated, especially when the sign is on or turbulence is possible. Follow crew instructions.

02 · Exit awareness🚪

Locate your exits

Notice the nearest exits and remember they may be behind you. Count rows if useful; lighting and exit markings can help in reduced visibility.

03 · Oxygen🫁

Oxygen mask

If masks deploy, pull one toward you, place it over nose and mouth, secure it, and breathe normally. Assist yourself before helping others.

04 · Water safety🦺

Life vest awareness

Read the aircraft safety card for the exact model. On many aircraft, inflate the vest only after leaving the aircraft unless crew directs otherwise.

05 · Turbulence☁️

When turbulence hits

Return to your seat when instructed, buckle up, secure loose objects and keep aisles clear. Do not get up to retrieve overhead items during turbulence.

06 · Evacuation➡️

Evacuate without delay

Leave bags behind. Follow crew commands and illuminated paths, move away from the aircraft, and never use an exit if crew indicate it is unsafe.

Roles & teamwork

What cabin crew do

General public-level role overview; exact duties vary by airline, aircraft, route and training.

🧑‍✈️

Cabin crew member

Provides safety briefings, monitors the cabin, supports passengers, communicates concerns, and follows the airline’s approved procedures and crew coordination.

🛟

Safety & passenger support

Helps passengers understand safety information, supports people who need assistance, and reports hazards promptly through the appropriate chain.

📡

Team communication

Clear, calm communication and coordinated action matter. Crew use airline-specific training, checklists and command structures.

Knowledge boundary

Public guidance vs professional procedures

This site intentionally explains broadly known passenger precautions. It does not replace aircraft-specific safety cards, regulator requirements, airline manuals, recurrent training, or instructor-led emergency drills.

Always defer to the crew. Actual procedures vary by aircraft and airline. During a real flight, listen to the operating crew and follow the instructions given on board.
